# Drawn tiles are presented upright, and reorientation is a tip-over

A two-finger gripper cannot turn a face-up tile upright without either a horizontal approach, which drives the gripper housing into the table, or a 45-degree side grasp. In the YAM workspace the side grasp is reachable only near the base, and its pads clear the table by under a millimetre. So the table side presents drawn tiles (including Kong replacements) upright in the shared zone with the face toward the robot, where the wrist camera identifies them. Claimed discards are presented face-up, as they lie in the pond. The robot therefore reorients in only one direction, upright to face-up, by standing the tile on the table and tipping it backward onto its back. Every discard and every meld tile taken from the hand needs this.

Holding hand tiles across their thickness leaves their sides free. Insertion therefore lowers a tile straight into the gap its extraction left, or against the end of the row, with no finger between tiles. When a turn removes more tiles than it inserts (Chow, Pung, Kong), a compaction push closes the remaining gaps so the hand stays close-packed.

## Consequences

- The five gate-two skills are realised as follows. Shared-zone pick takes either presentation. Close-packed extraction grips across thickness from above. Insertion goes into a gap or against the row end. Reorientation is the tip-over. Placement puts a face-up tile into a pond or meld slot.
- Gate four's human-assistance protocol must present draws upright and face toward the robot (ADR 0003).
- Compaction is a contact operation that the gate-two skill list does not name; it is counted within extraction.
